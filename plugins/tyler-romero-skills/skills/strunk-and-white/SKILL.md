# Strunk and White

Use the relevant guidance in *The Elements of Style*: [Chapter II](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/37134/37134-h/37134-h.htm#Page_7) for usage and punctuation, [Chapter III](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/37134/37134-h/37134-h.htm#Page_15) for composition, [Chapter IV](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/37134/37134-h/37134-h.htm#Page_33) for matters of form, and [Chapter V](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/37134/37134-h/37134-h.htm#Page_36) for word choice. Apply their advice where it improves the passage, rather than treating every preference in the book as a rule.

- Preserve the author's meaning, factual claims, intended audience, and useful voice. Keep technical terms, quotations, citations, and deliberate stylistic choices unless the user asks to change them.
- Give each paragraph a clear purpose. Improve the order of ideas and use a topic sentence when it helps the reader follow the argument.
- Prefer direct, affirmative sentences and active voice when they make the actor and action clearer. Keep passive voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or less important than the action.
- Replace vague or abstract wording with specific, concrete language when the source supports it. Do not invent details to make prose sound more specific.
- Omit needless words and repetition without removing necessary nuance. Keep related words together, express comparable ideas in parallel form, and place emphasis where it serves the point.
- Correct grammar and punctuation in the convention of the document. When presentation is in scope, make headings, numerals, quotations, references, and titles consistent with the document's style. Treat period-specific usage and formatting advice in the linked edition as context, not a mandate for modern prose.

Return the edited text in the user's requested format. For a straightforward edit, provide the revision directly; call out only substantive changes, unresolved ambiguity, or places where preserving meaning requires the author's decision.
